Vienna's coffeehouse culture is usually dated to the 1680s, after the siege of 1683, when the first licensed houses opened near the Stephansdom. They became places to read the papers, play chess and stay for hours over a single cup.

By the nineteenth century there were well over a hundred, and writers, composers and politicians kept regular tables. A glass of water arrived with every coffee, and refills came unasked. Café Landtmann opened in 1873.

The houses declined after the wars but survived, and in 2011 the culture was listed as intangible heritage. The marble tables, newspapers on wooden holders and unhurried waiters are still there.
